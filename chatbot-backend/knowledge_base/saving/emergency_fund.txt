Title: Building and Managing an Emergency Fund

An emergency fund is a dedicated, easily accessible reserve of cash intended solely for unexpected, urgent, and necessary expenses. It serves as a financial shock absorber, preventing reliance on high-interest credit cards, personal loans, or premature withdrawal from retirement funds.

Essential Rules for Emergency Funds:
1. Target Size:
- Starter Emergency Fund: 1 month of essential expenses or a set baseline (e.g., $1,000 to $2,000 / Rs. 25,000 to Rs. 50,000) while actively paying down high-cost toxic debt.
- Fully Funded Reserve: 3 to 6 months of mandatory living expenses (rent, food, basic utilities, loan obligations, insurance).
- High Uncertainty Profile: 6 to 12 months if self-employed, working on commission, or in single-income households with multiple dependents.

2. Qualified Emergency Uses:
- Sudden medical emergencies or hospital bills
- Urgent, unavoidable home or automobile repairs necessary for daily livelihood
- Job loss or income interruption

3. Unqualified Uses (Not Emergencies):
- Vacation travel, entertainment events, impulse electronics purchases
- Predictable periodic expenses (annual insurance premiums or festival gifts, which belong in regular sinking funds)

4. Location & Accessibility:
Keep emergency reserves in safe, liquid accounts such as high-yield savings accounts or money market accounts where funds can be accessed within 24 hours without penalty.
