Title: The 50/30/20 Budgeting Rule

The 50/30/20 rule is an intuitive, proportional budgeting framework designed to help individuals manage their after-tax income effectively across three primary categories:

1. Needs (50%):
Approximately 50% of your net income should cover essential living expenses. These are non-negotiable costs required for basic survival and daily operations. Common examples include:
- Housing costs (rent or mortgage payments)
- Groceries and basic nutrition
- Utilities (electricity, water, heating, basic internet)
- Transportation (commute costs, public transit, fuel, basic vehicle maintenance)
- Healthcare and essential medical prescriptions
- Minimum required debt payments

2. Wants (30%):
Up to 30% of your net income may be allocated toward discretionary spending that enhances your lifestyle but is not essential to survival:
- Dining out at restaurants and cafes
- Entertainment, movie tickets, concerts, and streaming subscriptions
- Hobbies, vacations, and leisure travel
- Luxury shopping, premium electronics, and non-essential clothing

3. Savings and Debt Repayment (20%):
At least 20% of your income should be directed toward building long-term financial security and freedom:
- Funding an emergency savings reserve
- High-interest debt payoff (credit cards, personal loans)
- Long-term investments and retirement contributions (401k, IRA, mutual funds, provident funds)

Flexibility Note:
In regions or life stages with high living expenses or student loans, the percentages can be adapted (e.g., 60/20/20 or 50/20/30) depending on individual income levels and personal goals.
